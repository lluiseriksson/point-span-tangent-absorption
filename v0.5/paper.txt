The proof gives two nonexclusive alternatives. Either Z contains d + 2 projectively
independent supports whose double points impose independent conditions, or Z lies in a
common projective tangent d-plane and in one fiber of the original Gauss map. In the
second branch, a characteristic-zero minimal-degree argument shows that the points are
unisolvent in degree m on that plane. Consequently the stronger bound (d + 1)(d + 2) is
unconditional for d ≥ 3, and also for d = 2, m ≥ 4. For d = 2, m = 3, the exact minimum
is 10, attained by a triangular lattice of ten points in a linearly embedded plane. Three
points on a linearly embedded P1 for m = 2 show that the degree assumption cannot be
weakened in the uniform theorem. A separate consequence of standard jet-ampleness gives
a further lower bound that grows linearly with m and eventually improves the constant
branch.

First jets and absorption

For a smooth point p ∈ X, let 2p be the first infinitesimal neighborhood defined by m2p . It
has length d + 1. Since H m is very ample, restriction to 2p is surjective. Intrinsically, its dual
is the affine tangent space to the cone over the H m -embedding; this description avoids any
coordinate-dependent scalar in a symmetric-tensor formula.
Definition 2.1. A nonempty finite reduced set Z ⊂ X is point-span tangent-absorbing with
respect to H m if Tbφm (p) φm (X) ⊆ SZ for every p ∈ Z.
Lemma 2.2 (Kernel criterion). The following are equivalent:
(i) Z is point-span tangent-absorbing;
(ii) equation (2) holds;
(iii) restriction to Z and restriction to 2Z have the same image rank.
Proof. The annihilator of SZ is the kernel of restriction to Z. The annihilator of the span of
the affine tangent spaces is the kernel of restriction to 2Z. Since the point span is contained in
the tangent span, the three conditions are equivalent.

Exact examples and the degree threshold

Proposition 7.1 (Exact plane-cubic minimum). For a linearly embedded X = P2 and m = 3,
the minimum cardinality and minimum vector-span dimension of a nonempty reduced point-span
tangent-absorbing set are both 10. Equality is attained by
Z = {[1 : i : j] : i, j ∈ Z≥0 , i + j ≤ 3}.
Proof. The lower bound 10 is Corollary 1.2(iii). The displayed set has ten points. To prove
that its evaluations span H 0 (P2 , O(3))∗ , dehomogenize and let f (x, y) have total degree at
most three and vanish on the triangular lattice.
On the layer y = 0, the polynomial f (x, 0) has the four roots 0, 1, 2, 3, hence vanishes
identically and f = yf1 with deg f1 ≤ 2. On y = 1, the polynomial f1 (x, 1) has the three
roots 0, 1, 2, so f1 = (y − 1)f2 with deg f2 ≤ 1. On y = 2, f2 (x, 2) has the two roots 0, 1, so
f2 = (y − 2)f3 with f3 constant. The last point on y = 3 forces f3 = 0. Thus f = 0.
The evaluation map between two ten-dimensional vector spaces is an isomorphism. Hence
the point span is the full ambient dual and contains every tangent space. Therefore Z is
absorbing and dim SZ = |Z| = 10.

Relation to adjacent interpolation notions

Terracini loci parameterize finite sets for which the union of double points fails to impose the
expected independent conditions [2, 6]. Point-span absorption is more restrictive: the value
rank and the double-point rank are equal. It therefore produces an extreme Terracini defect
whenever the value span is not already ambient, but ordinary Terracini defect does not imply
absorption.
The independence lemma belongs to the established theory of simultaneous jet separation.
In the terminology of Beltrametti–Di Rocco–Sommese [3], jet ampleness asks for uniform
surjectivity over prescribed zero-dimensional data. Their Proposition 2.3 implies that H m is
m-jet ample and is exactly the external input used in Corollary 1.4. By contrast, the proof of
Lemma 3.1 separates only its specific finite union of first neighborhoods. Ballico–Chiantini’s
condition † and their Theorem 3.5 provide exactly the product criterion used in Lemma 3.1.
Base loci and strong base loci of tangential projections concern spans generated by tangent
spaces that contain an additional point or an additional tangent space [1]. There is an exact

8

translation in one direction. Since the evaluation at p lies in the affine tangent space at φm (p),
absorption gives
SZ = Span{Tbφm (p) φm (X) : p ∈ Z}.
If A ⊆ Z is minimal among subsets whose tangent spaces span SZ , then every q ∈ Z \A belongs
to the strong base locus of A for φm (X). The extra feature here is that this tangent span
equals the span of evaluations of the full support Z. The Veronese classifications in [1] therefore
give relevant context, but do not by themselves establish the point-span bounds proved above.
That source also shows that strong base loci and Terracini loci are not comparable in general.
Finally, the exceptional branch uses the Gauss map of the original embedding, not the
Gauss map after the mth re-embedding. General fibers of Gauss maps in characteristic
zero and the additional inseparable phenomena in positive characteristic belong to a broader
classical theory; see, for example, [4, 5]. No classification of Gauss-degenerate varieties or their
absorbing configurations is attempted here.
These comparisons delimit the claims. They do not establish that no related statement
exists elsewhere, and no exhaustive priority assertion is made.

Scope and limitations

• Theorem 1.1 is stated and proved over C. The minimal-degree derivative step in
Lemma 5.1 is the specific obstruction to carrying this proof unchanged to positive
characteristic.
• The theorem bounds rank and cardinality but does not classify absorbing sets, even
inside the common Gauss fiber of alternative (b).
• The plane and three-space equality examples fill their ambient value spaces. No assertion
is made that all equality cases are ambient or that the examples classify equality.
• The comparison with the cited literature is deliberately conservative. It supplies attribution and logical distinctions, not a comprehensive bibliographic or priority review.
• Finite computational rank checks can verify explicit fixtures but do not prove the
universal theorem. The proof is the argument in Sections 3–6.

11

Conclusion

Point-span tangent absorption forces two complementary forms of rigidity. Support escape
produces d + 2 independent first-jet blocks, while failure of escape concentrates the set in a
common tangent plane and forces degree-m unisolvence there. Combining the two branches
yields the minimum in (5), the unconditional strong range in Corollary 1.2, and the exact
plane-cubic value 10. Jet-ampleness supplies the complementary growing estimate J(d, m) of
Corollary 1.4. The argument also isolates why characteristic zero and the threshold m ≥ 3
enter the present formulation.
